1  ·  A figure, drawn instead of plotted

drawn — p5.brush, painted lettering
original — matplotlib

Same numbers, read from 16_v3m_depth_targets.json in the latent_thought_monitorability repo. Not traced off this PNG. The drawn version dodges the three scopes apart on x, which the original cannot do without the whiskers stacking into a ladder.

Every label here is painted, not typeset: the glyph outlines are pulled from the font and filled with the same brush that drew the axes, so the lettering has the same bristle and paper tooth as everything else on the page.

2  ·  Background art: dry vs wet

first pass — mostly drawing
repainted — wet

The first plate was a drawing with colour behind it. The second inverts that: paint carries the image and the pencil only annotates. There is no watercolour brush in p5.brush 2.1.0-beta — brush.box() lists eleven, all dry media — so the wetness comes from the fill system: high bleed, dozens of overlapping translucent touches laid along the trajectory, pigment pooling at the rims, the drawing put underneath and glazed over, and a cold-press tooth multiplied across the sheet at the end.

3  ·  Coming next: orth-dion, from W&B

The eight LLaMA 320M validation-loss curves behind val_loss_curves.png were not on disk anywhere, so they now come out of the neurips_v4 W&B project via extract_orth_dion_val_loss.py. The numbers confirm the paper's claim cleanly: Orth-Dion beats Dion at every rank fraction (3.3074 vs 3.3121, 3.3406 vs 3.3466, 3.3781 vs 3.3824), with Muon lowest at 3.2606. The drawing itself is not built yet.

4  ·  What the style dial looks like

  • Closer to a paper. What you see above: real axes, real ticks, a legend. Reads as a figure that happens to be hand-drawn.
  • Further toward the notebook. Looser axes, gridlines sketched rather than ruled, more margin annotation in your own words, less frame. Reads as a page from a lab book.

Both come off the same kit, so this is a parameter choice, not a rewrite. Worth settling before the remaining figures get converted.
