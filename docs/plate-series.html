Plate series

Now live on the site. Kept as the record of what was decided and why. The question is whether a set of painted mathematical plates can carry the whole site the way one attractor carries the hero, and where each one would sit.

The idea

The attractor works because it is three things at once: scientific (a real dynamical system, annotated like a specimen), artistic (wet paint on cold-press paper), and yours (it is the motif your site already used). A series has to hold all three. So every plate below is a real mathematical object drawn from your own work, painted in one hand, with one shared grammar: corner ticks at the top left, a scale bar at the bottom left, leader lines out to the right margin. The subject changes, the handwriting does not.

The other half of the idea is restraint. Five plates used everywhere is wallpaper. Three used deliberately, on bare paper elsewhere, reads as intentional.

1  ·  The plates

Strange attractor

home hero

The Lorenz system: deterministic, bounded, and never repeating. Already your site's motif. Organic and sprawling, which is why the others are deliberately not.

Geometry of steepest descent

muon-geometry · portfolio

Three unit balls over one origin — L2 round, L∞ square, L1 diamond — with a single gradient and the point on each boundary that maximises the inner product. Three different answers from the same gradient: that is the entire reason SGD, signSGD and spectral methods take different steps, and it is the schematic behind the Muon layer criterion. Hard-edged on purpose, as the counterweight to the attractor.

Singular spectrum with a rank cut

orth-dion

A comb of decaying values and the line where you stop keeping them. Left of the cut is the subspace a low-rank optimizer actually carries; right of it is the tail it throws away, painted as ghosts. Dion and Orth-Dion both turn on whether that tail was really negligible and whether the kept basis stayed orthogonal. Rhythmic and vertical, so it sits differently on a page from everything else.

A chain of thought gone quiet

latent-cot · confessions

Spoken steps are pigment; latent ones are empty outlines on bare paper, because the absence is the subject and must not be painted. The thread goes dashed where it goes silent, and a probe reaches up into the gap. This is the clearest of the set: you can read the research question off it without a caption. Its strip composition makes it the natural wide banner.

Loss surface with a saddle

behind text

Real level sets of f(x,y) = (x²−1)² + 2y² − xy: two basins split by a saddle, with a trajectory integrated down the gradient from a bad initialisation and streamlines showing the field it fell through. It fills a page more evenly than the others, which is exactly what you want behind text rather than beside it.

Task arithmetic

task-arithmetic-fairness

Two fine-tuning difference vectors from a base model, and the corner you land on by adding both without further training. The angle at the origin is the whole story: near-orthogonal vectors compose cleanly, and the more they align or oppose, the more adding one distorts the other. Pans: ultramarine, rose, sepia.

Two hidden objectives

hidden-objectives

Two adapters trained to conceal different things, drawn as two supports. Where they overlap the pigment doubles and the region is hatched — the only hatching in the series, so it carries weight. Two separately trained probes converge on that patch, which is the question: is concealment one shared mechanism? Pans: madder, sap, sepia — the least comfortable pairing in the box, for the least comfortable question.

Search tree

materials-agents

A planner expanding candidates, killing most of them, and committing to one line. Most of the picture is abandoned work, struck through, with a single wet corridor from root to result — which is the honest shape of search. Pans: ochre, payne, sepia.

2  ·  The pigment box

Five plates in one palette is monotone — you scroll and nothing changes. But letting each invent its own colours breaks the series. Real watercolour work solves this the way a painter does: one box, a different selection per painting. Paper and pencil never change. The pans do.

5  ·  Rules that keep it clean

  1. One subject per plate. No decorative extras. If a mark is not part of the mathematics or the annotation grammar, it comes out.
  2. One accent hue per plate, over shared neutrals. Related, never identical.
  3. Paint never crosses the text column. Each plate is composed with a deliberately empty side.
  4. The same grammar every time — corner ticks, scale bar, leaders right. This is what makes five images a series instead of five pictures.
  5. Behind text ≤ 60% opacity; a standalone figure sits at 100%.
  6. Static. No motion, no parallax, no scroll effects.

6  ·  The chrome is too hard for the paint

Worth flagging now even though it comes after the plates. The paint is wet, deckled and irregular; the interface around it is Inter at border-radius: 2px with 1px solid borders. Those are two different worlds on one page, and the mismatch will get more obvious as the paint gets better.

A rough CSS approximation, not the real thing. What actually changes:

  1. Kill the 1px borders. A hairline rectangle is the single most software-looking element on the page. Replace with a faint paper-tinted ground and no edge at all.
  2. Stop rounding corners. 2px radius reads as neither crisp nor soft. Paper does not have rounded corners — it has irregular ones. The same brush that paints the plates can render a deckle edge as an asset, which is the honest fix.
  3. Buttons become underlines. A bordered rectangle is a UI control; a pencil rule under a word is a note. The rule can be brush-drawn from this pipeline so it has real graphite texture.
  4. Warm the type. Inter and Space Grotesk are geometric grotesques — excellent and cold. A humanist serif for body (Newsreader, Literata, Source Serif) would sit with the paper instead of against it. You already load Caveat for .handwritten; that hand should appear more, not less.
  5. Images sit on the page, not in frames. Drop the border, add a faint soft shadow so a plate reads as laid on the sheet.
  6. Rules and dividers get drawn. <hr> becomes a brush-rendered pencil line rather than a CSS border.

Started: see the softening pass for a before/after, and public/soft.css for the drop-in layer. Points 2, 3 and 6 all produce small PNG or SVG assets from the same renderer that makes the plates — the interface would literally be drawn with the same pencil. That is what would make it cohere rather than merely match.

8  ·  Costs, honestly

  • Each plate takes 2–3 minutes to render and lands around 300–900KB. Site-wide they should be down-ressed to display size and served as WebP, or a post with several gets heavy.
  • Renders are not reproducible byte-for-byte — p5.brush carries its own randomness. p5.brush 2.2.2 adds seed(), which would fix that; the pipeline can already switch with --p5brush 2.2.2.
